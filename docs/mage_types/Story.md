# Intro
Note: the player character's canonical name will be Ally. The player can change this, of course, but that'll be the default name for now. 

The player comes home to their apartment from a long day at work. They look inside the fridge for something to eat, only to notice an odd takeout container. It is simply labelled "Diner: Just off the Cyan Interstate." The player character has heard of neither this generic diner nor of this interstate. They take a bite of the pancakes, noting they taste good, and head into their room. However, once crossing the threshold, they find themselves in an unfamiliar stairwell. They look around, clearly confused.
>We might have to railroad the player to ensure they open the fridge.

The player is able to explore the stair well, not that there's much to explore. There will be at most 3 levels, but likely just one. The stairs will wrap around. Maybe the other levels can be unlocked during the game and will lead to other locations on Forlorn.

Exiting the stairwell will take the player to the lighthouse, another confusing transition. The door will lock behind them, forcing them down the pier. On the beach, the player will find a caretaker, a man in the late stages of Blue-alignment, rooted in place. With him is *Alice*, the player's first companion. Alice and the player will get to know each other, exchanging 3 small talk questions. The player's responses to these questions will determine Alice's *fated bias*.

Upon asking the man about his condition, he will explain the concept of *alignment*, what it is and that it's an inevitable fact of life on Forlorn. Alice is horrified by the concept, due in no small part to the man's horrifying condition. The man suggests she and the player should expose themselves to each element, which is known to help stave off alignment, at least for a little bit. Then, if Alice really finds the idea unbearable, she should seek out *Mr. Beau*, a man who has been unaligned for almost 10 years. However, the man does not know of Mr. Beau's location, suggesting Alice seeks out someone in the Magenta Community Center.

The player character finds their mind transfixed on the diner, although whether this is due to them connecting it to their current situation or because the food was really good is up to the player. They can ask the man about either the diner or the Cyan Interstate. He says it sounds familiar, but confesses he has not been on the surface in years and his memory is foggy.

Before Alice and the player leaves, the man asks them to keep an eye out for a boy named *Alex*. Evidentally, he came through here about a week ago, but seemed a little airheaded, so the man worries he has gotten lost. As the player and Alice ascend through the Blue Caverns, they do happen to stumble into Alex. Alice/Ally asks them if they're okay, have they really been lost down here for an entire week! To which Alex replies, "no, I was only lost down here for 3 days. I finally made it to the surface, found the community center, and was chilling out around there. Then, an hour ago I walked into what I thought was a maintenance closet and found myself back here."
# Main Character Motivations
Alice wants to avoid alignment, but finds one of the elements alluring. She wants to talk to Mr. Beau to ask him for advice on avoiding this fate.

Alex loves this new place he found. His home life was bad and he spent much of his youth wishing he could be anywhere else. His life had just turned around, things were starting to improve, then he found himself here. He finds the magic and setting awesome, but grieves the lost possibilities. He wants to explore, but would like the Ally & Alice's help, as to not get lost (again).

The player is tasked with finding the Diner, which Ally is obsessing over. Whether this is a whimsical adventure to taste those amazing pancakes again or a desperate attempt to get home is up to the player, via dialog options and their personal roleplaying.
# Major Actors
## Blue Enclaves
The Enclaves are small groups of 2-6 Blue-aligned who are rooted in place within talking distance of each other. These enclaves could give the player small quests, like finding items for them or passing messages from one enclave to the other.
## The Great Enclave(s)
The shattered remanants of the once ~50 strong Great Enclave conflict with one another, each believing their own chapter to be the true successors to the Great Enclave. There are three of these successor enclaves, which the player can choose to ally themselves with. Theoretically, the player could ally themselves with all three, as the groups have no way of verifying the player's loyalty.
## Parking Garage
The player can venture into the parking garage early into the game, although all but the first level is more of a late-game area. Here they'll have to fight off Purple-aligned, wraiths (Purple-aligned that have lost themselves), and various monsters.

On the first level, the player can enter the convenience store, meeting the Clerk and some Purple mages. The Purple mages offer the player some advice and gives them a quest concerning a generator on the second level. On this level will be a powerful monster that the player cannot tackle directly, even at a higher level, and must therefore sneak around. The mages don't actually expect you to be able to accomplish this and are impressed if you do.
## The Community Center
The main conflict in the community center has to do with the boarded up rooms, used to contain rogue monsters. These restricted areas have grown to consume half of the center's area, allowing many of these once isolated containment zones to connect, leading to a rapidly growing threat. The core leadership seems to be in denial of this fact, though their opposition (the peacekeepers) are quickly gaining support.
## The Peacekeepers
Initially a simple security force used to contain the monsters raging inside the Center, a change in leadership has taken them down a darker path. An exiled Green-aligned mage has unseated the previous chief of security and is trying to maintain his power through the chaos.
## The Gardeners
Recently a valuable medicinal plant was stolen, an action the Gardeners blame on Green. The newfound leader of the peacekeepers has stoked these concerns, hoping to exacerbate the already shaky relationship between the two, in order to cover up the true reason for their exile.

# Main Plot

>[!note] Themes
>The major theme in this game is the corruptive and transformative nature of power. This takes the form of both the magic system, as well as power in a more mundane sense.

The player will be fairly free to explore and help the factions as they see fit. Each faction will have its own conflict/plot, allowing the player to choose sides, etc. 

The main plot will center around the conflict between Green and Magenta. There has long been resentment between the two groups, but tensions have recently flared. An exiled Green mage (*Laurel*) is stirring up resentment against the leadership of the Community Center, who haven't done much to address the backroom monsters. This marks a shift in sentiment towards Green, as their decisive action seems welcome. However, the fact that this popular mage was exiled speaks badly for the faction as a whole.

Furthermore, the Gardeners, tasked with maintaining the Greenhouse, have recently lost a valuable plant and blame Green for stealing it. Though initially it will seem like Green are the antagonists, talking with their faction will reveal the situation to be much more complicated. The faction did steal from the Gardeners, an action fuelled by a desperate need for the healing properties of the stolen plant. This need could have been dealt with diplomatically, but communication between the two factions has always bordered on hostile. As far as the Laurel is concerned, the less contact between Magenta and Green, the less likely people will be to learn of his past crimes.

Gaining the trust of Green will reveal the other side of this plot. As it turns out, Laurel is wanted for essentially being a serial killer. Part of the reason the medicinal plant was stolen from Magenta was to heal one of the surviving victims of their greivous injuries. Green tried to warn Magenta of the danger, but were rudely rebuffed, now opting to let things play out.

Meanwhile, the old Peacekeeper second in command, *Rose*, is trying to overthrow Laurel and take control herself. Both Rose and Laurel try to enlist the help of the player, asking for them to ask Cyan or Red for help respectively. Depending on who the player helps, one side will come out victorious, setting their sights on Green. Canonically, it will be Rose who wins, lending to the theme of power corrupting. However, the storyline will play out fairly similarly regardless of who wins. Learning of an ancient mystical artifact, Rose (or Laurel) will begin to search for it, the paranoia and lust for power spiraling them out of control.

As their madness progresses, Rose/Laurel descend into the maintenance halls/Catacombs, searching for this weapon. The player chases after them, fighting their way through fanatical Peacekeepers (the less devout having deserted). This chase culminates in Rose/Laurel transforming into a terrible beast and fighting the player's party. 

The following factors will have an effect on the ending:
- If the player befriends Lime, Green will join the fight in the halls/Catacombs, occupying a third of the Peacekeeper fanatics. Similarly, if the player has completed the Ranger quests, they too will join the fight, occupying another third of the Peacekeepers. 
- Befriending Lime also comes with the benefit of her immense combat strength.
- If Laurel won and the player has aligned to Red or Alice's fated bias is Red, they'll have an easier time navigating the Catacombs and will beat Laurel there.
- If Rose won and the player has aligned to Purple or Alice's fated bias is Purple, they'll have an easier time navigating the maintenance halls and will beat Rose there.
- If the player has completed the Hunter quest and met Last, he will join the final confrontation, before Rose/Laurel transform. Instead of simply being unable to find the weapon, they learn that Last threw it over the side of the island.

Act 1 officially starts when the player enters the Magenta Community Center. Here, they will meet the various related factions. They'll be given a quest to venture into the forest to look for medicinal plants, so that the loss of the stolen plant can be lessened. To fulfill this quest, the player must meet with the Rangers. They are also given a quest by the Peacekeepers to do reconnaisance on a distant part of the Center. Completing these two quests will start act 2, however the player will also be given a major quest by the Rangers. The player can also reach the Diner, but will find it closed, as the Cook is catering to a party in the Tower. From here, they can technically reach Orange and Cyan, but will be significantly underleveled. But this means that they can start the Hunter subplot and find Lime. 
- If Lime is in the player's party when they try to enter the Community Center, she will leave out of anxiety, returning to the powerplant.
- The player is also able to ascend the Tower and find the Cook, but they will not return to the Diner until act 2. When the player reaches them, the party will be over, the food eaten, and the Cook 'cleaning up.'

During act 2, the player will be approached by Laurel and Rose (separately), who each gives them a quest to enlist the help of Red/Cyan. Both also mention a Green conspiracy against them and ask the player to avoid the powerplant. Entering Red or Cyan, the player is given a quest to complete before they can ask for that faction's help. Alternatively, the player can ignore Rose and Laurel, visiting Green. Here, they'll be given a task to find Lime, who is somewhere in Cyan. Completing either the Cyan or Red questline will the player to enlist their help. Only Cyan or Red can be enlisted. Lime can now join the player's party and will not leave upon entering the Center. The player should also be a high enough level to take on the Orange Campus, unlocking the Hunter subplot.
- Lime can now officially join the player's party.
- Player can complete the Ranger and Orange Campus subplots at their leisure.
- The player has full access to the map, except for the Catacombs and maintenance tunnels.
- The Cook returns to the diner, allowing Ally to finish their personal objective.

Once the player finishes the enlistment arc and returns to the Center act 3 will begin. Here, they will find the battle has already begun. Either Cyan or Red mages will be fighting the Peacekeepers. The player makes their way to where Laurel and Rose are fighting. Laurel is giving his villain monologue, revealing the existance of a powerful artifact that will 'deal with the Green problem once and for all.'  If the player chose to help Laurel, he will then leave you to 'clean up the mess,' runnning off to the Catacombs to search for the weapon. Rose accuses you of betraying her and a boss fight begins. If instead the player helped Rose, Laurel will incapacitate her mid sentence, upon noticing the player's party, knocking her out instantly. The boss fight then begins.

The supposed location of this artifact will change based on who the player helped. If this was Laurel, he will already be at the Catacombs. Otherwise, Rose will come to and thank you for your help. She will then begin muttering about the artifact Laurel mentioned was in the Purple maintenance halls. She makes her way into a nearby closet, entering the same halls Alex previously got lost in. Several loyal Peacekeepers follow her inside. 

The player can choose when to start this final confrontation, allowing them to finish any of their other side plots. I'd prefer that the game not reset once the player finishes the main story (e.g. once the boss battle concludes, the player is sent back to their previous save).
